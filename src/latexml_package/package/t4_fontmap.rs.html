1//! T4 font encoding: the fc ("African Computer") fonts of t4enc.def (fc package, Jörg Knappen).
2//!
3//! The lower half is T1's (the fc fonts are EC-shaped: fcaccent.mf, fcpunct.mf, fcrlig.mf draw T1's accents,
4//! quotes, dashes and ligatures at T1's slots). The upper half is fccoding.mf's African Latin letters, each decoded to
5//! the Unicode letter fccoding.mf's glyph name and t4enc.def's input command name (`\m{y}` hooktop y, slot 175 →
6//! ƴ), checked against fc.rme's Appendix D "T4-to-UCS correspondence" — whose slips are not followed: 139 Ń (the
7//! table's U+0134 is Ĵ), 150 Ʒ (U+0167 ŧ), 152 Ė (U+0166 Ŧ), 162 ɛ (U+0258 ɘ, the open e of `\m e`), 172 ʃ (U+03A3 Σ),
8//! 173 ŋ / 174 ʋ (swapped), 226 ɛ̃ (ɘ̃). The letters with no precomposed character (fc.rme: E̱ O̱ e̱ o̱, Ɛ̃ Ɔ̃ ɛ̃ ɔ̃) and the
9//! fj ligature are multi-character slots; the accents at 159 (`\G`), 188 (`\U`), 191 (`\I`) and the Polish stroke at
10//! 223 (`\L`, `\l`: t4enc.def:62-74) are their combining marks.
11//!
12//! Perl has no T4 map, and its LaTeX.pool `\DeclareTextComposite` is ignored, so neither engine printed `\m{y}`: the
13//! encoding fell back to OT1, the composite was never declared, and the command reached t4enc.def's own
14//! `\errmessage` (2405.01972: 44 Errors, the letters lost; pdflatex 0). OXIDIZED_DESIGN_DIVERGENCES #777.
