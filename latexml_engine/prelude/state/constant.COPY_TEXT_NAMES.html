The text names a stand-in’s copy may supply over its binding, the kernel’s default (OXIDIZED_DESIGN_DIVERGENCES #731 rules 13 and 14; user ruling 2026-10-21: “the bindings should always win in terms of semantic markup … the slight typographic variations in the raw .cls or .sty file … if we can preserve and reuse them that should be via a generic mechanism that is set in the binding with a reasonable default”): the standard classes’ and LaTeX’s \…name macros, the word a heading or a label prints — aip-cp.cls:875 \newcommand\refname{REFERENCES} (1506.04050, 1604.08112), the AASTeX 6+ copies’ \def\abstractname{ABSTRACT} (2107.03400), proto.cls:798 \def\abstractname{~} (1402.7103). A binding extends it with its own (declare_copy_text_names). Everything else a binding defines is its markup of the class, and the copy’s version of it is declined: caption formats (\fnum@<type>, whose punctuation met the binding’s caption separator, “Fig. 1.—.— “ in 1106.2565), the caption separator, \@thmcountersep (1803.06644’s “Theorem 51”), \the<ctr>, \p@<ctr>, \the<ctr>@ID, list labels (\labelitemi), \today, \baselinestretch, \CurrentOption, every \lx@ name, structure.
