latex.ltx:1245-1257 \@xargdef: a command with an optional argument is \@protected@testopt\cs\\cs{<default>}, and the macro reading [#1] and the rest is the internal \\cs, the name \csname\string\cs\endcsname reaches. Our \cs reads its optional argument itself (Perl’s convertLaTeXArgs), so the internal is defined beside it for code that calls it directly: the amsart idiom \edef\cs{\@dblarg\csname\string\cs\endcsname} (bcp.cls:151-153, cmfne.cls:460,483), whose \cs otherwise never stored its argument (witnesses 1510.00296, 1704.01336, 1207.2066, 2003.00661, 2210.02246). Every declarator built on \@xargdef has it: \newcommand, \renewcommand, \providecommand, the begin macro of \newenvironment/\renewenvironment (\@newenv’s \new@command, latex.ltx:1324-1331), and \DeclareRobustCommand’s inner \cs␣ (\new@command of it, :1412-1428; the internal is \\cs␣). A starred declarator’s internal is not \long either (\l@ngrel@x, :1229-1233). It reads […] as \@yargdef does here (an optional argument, where LaTeX’s requires the brackets). Not for an active character, whose \string names another control sequence (~’s would be \~). The null control sequence’s is \\csname\endcsname, as \string prints it (tex.web §263), never \\ (2410.07647; guard perfect_kernel_batch131fix::null_cs_optional_internal_keeps_the_line_break). A \renewcommand, \DeclareRobustCommand or \newenvironment the lock refuses still defines its internal, as \@xargdef does: only \csname\string\cs\endcsname reaches it, the class’s own code, and that is how the class’s refused setter is replayed (cmfne.cls:462-483 \renewcommand{\author}[2][] then the \@dblarg \edef, whose \firstshortauthor its \enddoc@text prints; 2210.02246). No binding defines a \\cs internal of its own (a locked one would refuse the class’s); twoopt’s and xargs’s declarators make the document’s, as their .sty files do. A locked \renewenvironment is refused whole, its stores recorded; a \newcommand/\providecommand of a defined name defines none, as LaTeX’s.
