The handlers of biblatex’s own field names (biblatex manual §2.2.2), each \bib@field@<type>@<field> that files its value in the entry’s structure. declared: None for every field — the biblatex binding’s, on both of biblatex’s routes (a .bbl’s entries, a .bib rendered without biber: \printbibliography falls back to \bibliography{…}, biblatex_sty.rs \biblatex@printbibliography) —, else only the fields the document’s .bst declares in its ENTRY list (bst_declared_fields), which are the fields bibtex(1) passes to the style.

The classic reader filed them as unprinted ltx:bib-data, losing about 7,000 words of reference text in 78 manuals of sweep 120 (publisher locations, journal names, access dates, addenda). Rust-only: Perl has no biblatex binding and prints no bibliography without a .bbl. Defined for every route (batches 56ie-57), they printed under natbib/BibTeX what the style never prints — “Accessed: 2025-08-15”, “Note: ⟨addendum⟩” where plainnat and apsrev4-1 declare neither field (arXiv 2602.10008, 2606.11822), and a journaltitle={\undefinedmacro Jt} raised Error:undefined —, where Perl keeps them as \bib@field@default@default’s raw bib-data; a style that declares one prints it (ACM-Reference-Format.bst:38 location, sn-mathphys.bst and mdpi.bst urldate), as here (OXIDIZED_DESIGN_DIVERGENCES #823). Guards perfect_kernel_batch78i::{natbib_biblatex_only_fields_are_raw_data, biblatex_prints_its_own_fields, bst_declared_biblatex_field_is_printed}. An entry giving both journal and journaltitle (or subtitle and titleaddon) gets both; biber rejects such entries.
