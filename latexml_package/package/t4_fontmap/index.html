T4 font encoding: the fc (“African Computer”) fonts of t4enc.def (fc package, Jörg Knappen).

The lower half is T1’s (the fc fonts are EC-shaped: fcaccent.mf, fcpunct.mf, fcrlig.mf draw T1’s accents, quotes, dashes and ligatures at T1’s slots). The upper half is fccoding.mf’s African Latin letters, each decoded to the Unicode letter fccoding.mf’s glyph name and t4enc.def’s input command name (\m{y} hooktop y, slot 175 → ƴ), checked against fc.rme’s Appendix D “T4-to-UCS correspondence” — whose slips are not followed: 139 Ń (the table’s U+0134 is Ĵ), 150 Ʒ (U+0167 ŧ), 152 Ė (U+0166 Ŧ), 162 ɛ (U+0258 ɘ, the open e of \m e), 172 ʃ (U+03A3 Σ), 173 ŋ / 174 ʋ (swapped), 226 ɛ̃ (ɘ̃). The letters with no precomposed character (fc.rme: E̱ O̱ e̱ o̱, Ɛ̃ Ɔ̃ ɛ̃ ɔ̃) and the fj ligature are multi-character slots; the accents at 159 (\G), 188 (\U), 191 (\I) and the Polish stroke at 223 (\L, \l: t4enc.def:62-74) are their combining marks.

Perl has no T4 map, and its LaTeX.pool \DeclareTextComposite is ignored, so neither engine printed \m{y}: the encoding fell back to OT1, the composite was never declared, and the command reached t4enc.def’s own \errmessage (2405.01972: 44 Errors, the letters lost; pdflatex 0).
